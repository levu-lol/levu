What the next dollar of exposure costs

Safe exposure is finite — bounded by what the book can actually absorb and what first-loss capital can cover. Rent is nearly free while there is room and becomes prohibitive as it runs out.

Why both lanes still look cheap

Both sit well below the kink, so both pay close to the floor. USDG at 42.7% and ETH at 52.0% are five basis points apart per hour — the ETH lane costs more because it is nearer its own, smaller ceiling.

Where it stops being cheap

Past 80% the curve turns. At full utilisation the rate is roughly forty times the floor, which is the point: the last increment of exposure should be refused by price before it has to be refused by a cap.

Who is paid

Rent goes to the parties carrying the lane's risk — 70% to underwriters, who take the first loss before the insurance fund does, 20% to insurance, 10% to the protocol. Both sides of the book pay it, because both consume capacity.

Where each lane sits

A lane is not listed or delisted, it moves. Leverage expands as liquidity deepens and contracts as it thins; a dying market goes reduce-only and waits for positions to wind down. Nothing is ever force-closed.
