Perps on tokens nobody else will list

The three things that are different, from where you sit.

The long tail actually has markets

Most tokens never get a perp anywhere, because listing one is a decision a committee makes over weeks. Here it happens when the market can support it — spot first, leverage once there is a book to lend against.

Leverage scales with the book

Not a tier somebody picked. A deep market carries tens of times, a thin one single digits, and it moves while you hold — so you are never quietly over-levered on a market that got worse overnight.

A dying market doesn't dump you

When liquidity fades, leverage comes off first and the market keeps trading at 1×. You get time to leave. Nobody force-closes the whole book into an empty one.

Leverage comes from the book, not a tier

No fixed number per market. What you can borrow is what the market can absorb.

Most exchanges pick a number per market and defend it. Here it is a consequence: what it would cost to close the largest position the market allows. A deep book is cheap to close, so it carries more. A thin one is not. And it moves while you hold, without anyone republishing a tier list.

Under $250k of resting depth there is no leverage at all. Not reduced leverage — none. A book that small cannot absorb a liquidation, and offering to lend against it would be offering a position nobody can close. The market still trades, at full collateral, until somebody quotes it properly.

For the same reason no single position may exceed a quarter of the book it would be closed in, however much capital is backing the market. Capital absorbs a loss; it does not fill an order.

And the number is yours, not the market's. The figure above is what a small position gets. Ask for a size that is large against the book and the leverage comes down with it — 33× at a thousand dollars, 24× at a million in the same market — because closing your position is what your margin is paying for. The ticket shows what you get before you commit to it, rather than quoting a headline and filling you at something else.

33× is where our fees put the ceiling today, not where the engine stops. Liquidation has to leave an account solvent on the fee alone, so the fee sets a floor under margin and a roof over leverage. Cut the fee and the roof rises — to 100× at a fee of zero, which is where the risk engine itself stops. We would rather quote the number we can actually honour.

The bottom of the ladder matters as much as the top. A market that has gone quiet drops to 1× and stays open at full collateral, instead of being closed on you. That is the difference between choosing when to exit and finding out you already have.

Why you can leave

The part worth checking before you fund anything.

  • Your collateral sits in a contract, not with us. We move positions, not balances.
  • You can withdraw without our help. Every position is committed on chain with a proof you can use yourself. If we vanish, you still get paid — that path is built and tested, not a promise.
  • Liquidation size is capped by what can actually be sold. Exposure is limited to what the book can absorb, and no single account may be more than a tenth of a market — so a liquidation is not a hole somebody else pays for.
  • The price is checked in your browser. The number in the hero is not our API. It is read from the chain by this page, and the terminal shows every source behind it.
Fair warning. These markets are new and the thresholds behind them are judgement so far, not history. Leverage is capped low on purpose while that is true. Small tokens move violently and 1× can still hurt.
